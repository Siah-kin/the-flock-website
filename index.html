Why we built The Flock

A lot of the internet has become a poor place to spend time. AI writes much of what we read, and bots farm the replies, so the people who actually explain a project get buried.

The good AI is the AI you don’t notice: AI in the human loop, making the human experience more intuitive and preventing the pitfalls that have been choking society. We want it to boost human creativity instead of farming it. It should help a person say what they mean in their own words, and point out what reads as farmed.

We built it for the OGs: the holders who stay loyal to a project beyond its price, and spend months teaching newcomers and pitching technology they believe in. Their heroics are rarely credited.

Quest platforms and raid bots get farmed.

When a tool pays for clicks, posts or messages, people can learn its rules and farm the rewards. The members who did useful work can get lost in that activity. The Flock adds a line to a member’s track record only when your team marks a request useful or reviews a post.

X no longer allows apps that pay people to post.

On 15 January 2026 X’s head of product wrote that X would “no longer allow apps that reward users for posting on X”, and blamed AI slop and reply spam. The same day, Kaito said it was shutting down Yaps, where people earned a score for posting about projects. So a community that pays per post, per reply or per cashtag raid now works against X’s own rules.

Anyone can become a professional and help pitch a project, out of loyalty or as a small business of their own. Both are fair when the work is real. But the method has to be forged, and that is where The Flock steps in.

How The Flock works with that rule

  • Nobody is paid to post, to reply or to raid a cashtag.
  • Posts are reviewed by a person for quality, never counted for volume.
  • Planned: points for help your team confirmed, which members later use to claim from a pool your project funds (how it works).
  • We discourage raids and spam, because they never get a project the result it needs.

Answers that show where they come from.

A wrong answer that sounds sure does more harm than no answer. So this bot is designed to show its evidence.

  • Your team approves the texts it may answer from, such as your docs and the wording on your app’s pages. It answers only from those.
  • Every answer shows the exact passage it used and the date of that source, so members can check it themselves.
  • When the sources don’t cover a question, it says so and offers a person on your team.
  • When your team edits or withdraws a source, the bot stops answering from it. The private test has to show this works before anything goes live.
  • During the test, a person reads every answer before it is sent.

The first test question is one members ask often: can I open a pool for a token that already exists?
